# Privacy Policy

> **DRAFT for review.** This is not final and has not been reviewed by a lawyer. Replace every [bracketed placeholder] before publishing.

**Effective date:** [DATE]

Dumb Luck is a coin-flip game made by [DEVELOPER NAME] ("we", "us"). This policy explains, in plain language, what happens to your information when you play.

## The short version

- There are no Dumb Luck accounts, and we don't run any servers. We don't collect your name, email or anything else about you ourselves.
- Your scores and settings are stored on your iPhone.
- If you use Game Center, Apple handles your scores and achievements.
- The free version shows ads from Google AdMob, which may collect device data to show and measure ads.
- If you buy "Remove Ads", Apple handles the payment. We never see your payment details.

## What stays on your device

The app saves your best streak, your settings (sound effects, music, haptics) and whether you've bought Remove Ads. This stays on your iPhone. We can't see it. Deleting the app deletes it, and you can reset your best streak any time in Settings.

## Game Center

Dumb Luck uses Apple's Game Center for leaderboards and achievements. If you're signed in to Game Center, your scores, achievements and Game Center nickname are sent to Apple and can be seen by other players (for example, on leaderboards and by your Game Center friends). Apple's [Privacy Policy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/) covers this. You can sign out of Game Center in your iPhone's Settings.

## Ads (Google AdMob)

The free version of Dumb Luck shows ads from Google AdMob. To show ads, limit how often you see them, measure how they perform and prevent fraud, Google may collect:

- device identifiers, including the advertising identifier (IDFA), but only if you allow tracking
- your IP address and approximate location derived from it
- information about your device (model, operating system, language)
- how you interact with ads (views and taps)
- crash and performance data related to ads

iOS asks whether you want to allow tracking (App Tracking Transparency). If you say no, the app can't access your advertising identifier and you'll see ads that aren't based on tracking you across other companies' apps and websites. You can change this any time in Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking. Where the law requires it (for example, in the EU and UK), we'll ask for your consent before ads use your data.

Google's use of this data is covered by [Google's Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy) and [how Google uses information from apps that use its services](https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites).

## Remove Ads purchase

You can pay $1.99 (or the local equivalent) once to remove ads. The purchase goes through Apple's App Store, under Apple's terms and privacy policy. Apple tells the app that you bought it, so the app can turn ads off. We never receive your name, Apple Account or payment details.

## Children

Dumb Luck isn't directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from them. If you think a child has provided information through the app, contact us and we'll help.

## What we don't do

- We don't sell your personal information.
- We don't have accounts, a sign-up form, or our own analytics or servers.
- We don't access your contacts, photos, camera, microphone or precise location. When you share a score card, iOS shares only the image you chose to share.

## Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll update this page and the date at the top. Big changes will also be mentioned in the app's update notes.
